# Module 2 — Praat / acoustic measurement basics

**Status: DRAFT (2026-08-05)** — content complete; awaiting PI review to flip to READY.

**Purpose.** Enough Praat to open sound + TextGrid, read a spectrogram, and hand-measure
vowel formants (F1/F2) — the skill that lets an RA **QC automated measurements** and trust
(or reject) them.

That framing is deliberate and worth taking literally. **You are not being trained to
hand-measure a corpus.** Nobody hand-measures 80,000 tokens. You are being trained to be
the person who can look at a number a script produced and say *"that one is wrong, and here
is why"* — because automated formant tracking fails in specific, recognizable ways, and a
pipeline with nobody checking it will publish those failures.

**~Time to complete:** 4–6 hours over ~2 weeks.

> **Data rule (from Module 0):** every exercise here uses **synthetic practice data only.**
> You do not open a real participant recording until Module 0 is passed and the PI has
> confirmed IRB status for your study. Raw audio never enters this repo — the practice
> materials are in `people/training/practice-data/praat/` and are generated by a script.

**Grounding and its limits.** Where this module states a lab setting, it cites the repo file
that documents it. **Almost every such file belongs to `p-oh-lowering`**, which is one
project's documented pipeline, not a ratified lab-wide standard. Whether those settings
carry over to your project is **`[confirm]`** with the PI. Where nothing in the repo
documents a convention, this module gives standard practice **explicitly labelled as a
starting default that needs PI ratification** — never as "the lab does X."

## 1. Install Praat; open a sound + TextGrid; navigate intervals

1. Install **Praat** (free, from the Praat website; it is a single application with no
   installer to speak of). [confirm: the lab pins no Praat version that this repo records.]
2. Praat has **two windows**: the **Objects** window (a list of things you have loaded) and
   the **Picture** window (drawing). You will live in Objects.
3. `Open > Read from file...` a `.wav`, then the matching `.TextGrid`. **Select both in the
   Objects list** (click one, ctrl/cmd-click the other) and click **View & Edit**. Selecting
   only the sound gives you a waveform with no annotation — the most common first-day
   confusion.
4. The editor shows the waveform, the spectrogram, and each TextGrid tier as a row of
   labelled intervals. **Tab** plays the visible part; clicking an interval selects it;
   the numbers under the window are the selection's start, end, and duration.

**What an aligned TextGrid looks like** (this is Module 3's subject, in one paragraph): a
`words` tier and a `phones` tier, where phone labels are ARPABET with a stress digit —
`AA1`, `IH0`, `EY2` — and `sil`/`sp` mark silence. The repo's own reader,
`projects/p-oh-lowering/analysis/replication/scripts/lib/textgrid_io.py`, expects exactly
that: tier names `phones` and `words`, and labels matching `^([A-Z]{2,3})([0-2])$`.

## 2. Reading a spectrogram; identifying vowel steady states

The spectrogram plots **time × frequency**, with darkness as energy. Formants are the dark
horizontal bands — resonances of the vocal tract. **F1 and F2 are what carry vowel
identity**, so they are what we measure.

The one intuition worth internalizing:

- **F1 tracks vowel height, inversely.** High vowels (FLEECE, GOOSE) have **low F1**; low
  vowels (TRAP, LOT) have **high F1**.
- **F2 tracks frontness.** Front vowels (FLEECE, DRESS) have **high F2**; back vowels
  (GOOSE, THOUGHT) have **low F2**.

That is why vowel plots put **F2 on a reversed x-axis and F1 on a reversed y-axis** — the
plot then looks like the mouth (see `r-for-lab-models.md` §3). It is also your first QC
instrument: *a high front vowel with a high F1 is not a high front vowel — it is a
measurement error.* You will use exactly that reasoning in Exercise 2.

**Finding the steady state.** A vowel's edges are contaminated by the consonants around it:
formants bend into and out of neighbouring stops and nasals. The measurable part is the
**steady middle**, where the tracks are flattest. Look for the interval where F1 and F2 stop
moving, and measure there — not at the boundary.

**What the repo documents about where to measure.** `p-oh-lowering`'s superseded
Parselmouth path trimmed each vowel to its **middle 20–80%** and took the **median across
analysis frames** (`config.yaml`: `trim_start: 0.20`, `trim_end: 0.80`; the median is in
`scripts/lib/formants.py`). Its live path instead uses FastTrack's smoothed-track sampling.
For **dynamic** vowels, trajectory measures (rather than a single point) may apply — which
measurement point convention applies to your project varies by design, so **ask before you
measure**. Lab-internal provenance for these defaults is tracked in the project records.

## 3. Measuring F1/F2 — settings, and the pitfalls

In the editor: **Formant > Show formants** overlays the tracker's estimates as dotted red
tracks. **Formant > Formant settings...** is where the tracker is configured, and
**Formant > Formant listing** prints values for the current selection.

### 3.1 The formant ceiling is the setting that matters

The **maximum formant (ceiling)** tells Praat the top of the range in which to look for a
fixed number of formants. Set it too low and the tracker crams formants together; set it too
high and it finds spurious ones and starts assigning F3 to the F2 slot. **It is the single
most consequential setting you will touch**, because it interacts with vocal-tract length:
speakers with shorter vocal tracts have higher formants and need a higher ceiling.

**Conventional adult starting values — ASSUMPTION, needs PI ratification:**

| | Typical ceiling |
|---|---|
| Adult male-range speaker | **5,000 Hz** |
| Adult female-range speaker | **5,500 Hz** |
| Children | higher still — no lab value is documented; `[confirm]` |

These two numbers do appear in the repo, at
`projects/p-oh-lowering/analysis/replication/config.yaml`
(`extraction.formant_ceiling_hz: {Male: 5000, Female: 5500}`, commented "standard
Praat/sociophonetic defaults"). **Read that carefully before quoting it:** per
`projects/p-oh-lowering/methods/extraction-and-measurement.md` §2.2, those fixed ceilings
belong to a **superseded** extraction path and **feed no reported number** in that paper.
They are a reasonable default to start from. They are **not** a ratified lab standard, and
this module does not present them as one.

Other Praat settings you will see, with the values that project documented:
`number of formants 5`, `window length 0.025 s`, `time step 0.005 s`,
`pre-emphasis from 50 Hz`. Standard, and rarely the thing that is wrong.

### 3.2 The cautionary tale — a tracking setting can flip a sign

A formant-tracking setting can determine the **sign** of a reported result — and even a claim
that important, made by your own lab, is **testimony until a committed artifact backs it up**.
Lab-internal provenance for the worked case behind both halves of that lesson is tracked in the
project records.


### 3.3 The four ways a tracker goes wrong

1. **It skips a formant.** What it reports as F2 is really F3 (and its F3 is really F4).
   Tell: F2 jumps far above the speaker's range for that vowel while **F1 stays normal**.
2. **It loses F1** — typically a ceiling problem, or low-amplitude/creaky voice. Tell: an
   impossible F1, e.g. a high front vowel with F1 near 700 Hz.
3. **There is not enough vowel to measure.** Very short tokens give the analysis window
   nothing stable. `p-oh-lowering` excludes tokens below **0.05 s** and above **0.5 s**
   (`config.yaml`: `min_duration_sec`, `max_duration_sec`). Whether those bounds apply to
   your project is `[confirm]`.
4. **Context, misread as error.** Nasalization, laterals, and rhotics genuinely move
   formants — a pre-nasal /æ/ really is higher and fronter. That is the vowel system doing
   something, not the tracker failing. **Never silently drop it.** Code the context and let
   the analysis handle it.

Pitfall 4 is the one that separates a useful RA from a destructive one. Dropping real
variation because it looked odd is how you delete your own result.

## 4. Building a hand-measured validation set

This is your actual deliverable on a project (see a Community-cluster work package). The
shape of it:

1. **Sample, don't cherry-pick.** Take a *random* subset — 20–30 tokens across speakers and
   vowel classes. Measuring the ones that look wrong tells you nothing about the rate.
2. **Measure blind** to the automated value where you can, so you are not anchored.
3. **Log every token**, agreements included, in `measurement-log.csv`. A validation set with
   only disagreements in it is not a validation set.
4. **Compare against a stated tolerance**, agreed in advance with the PI/methodologist.
5. **Investigate disagreements; do not silently exclude them.** Every exclusion gets a
   logged, criterion-based reason.
6. **Never hand-edit the machine-written output table.** If the pipeline is wrong, the
   pipeline gets fixed and re-run. A hand-corrected output file is an untraceable record —
   the lab treats that as worse than the error it fixes.

## 5. Pointer — parselmouth, for the same measurements in Python

`parselmouth` runs Praat's own analysis engine from Python, which is how the lab scripts it.
The repo's worked example is
`projects/p-oh-lowering/analysis/replication/scripts/lib/formants.py`: it extracts the
interval, calls `to_formant_burg(...)`, samples F1/F2 at each frame inside the trimmed
window, and takes the **median** (its docstring: "robust to occasional formant-tracker
glitches").

Read that file once, now that you know what the settings mean. You do not need to write
parselmouth code in this module — but recognizing that the script is doing exactly what you
just did by hand, with the same knobs, is the point.

Synthesize five vowels whose F1/F2 *you* choose, then measure them back. The ground truth is

**Exercise 2 — Find the mis-tracked tokens.**

Work speaker-by-speaker and vowel-by-vowel: a value is suspicious relative to *that
speaker's other tokens of that vowel*, not against an absolute number. Beware — **not every
odd-looking token is an error.**

## Answer key

**Ex. 1.** (a) **Pass = every vowel recovered within ±50 Hz on F1 and ±100 Hz on F2** of the
value you synthesized. *(Tolerance is a starting default — `ASSUMPTION`, `[confirm]` with
the PI.)* Clean synthesis should land well inside that; if you are outside it, you are
almost certainly measuring at a boundary rather than the steady middle, or the ceiling is
wrong.

(b) Expected: at a **3,000 Hz** ceiling the tracker has too little room and **merges or
mis-assigns the upper formants** — you will typically see F2 pulled toward the true F3, and
the front vowels (high true F2) degrade first. At **8,000 Hz** it looks for formants that
are not there and **inserts a spurious one**, pushing the reported F2 upward. The general
statement is what counts: *the ceiling determines which resonance lands in which slot.*

**Ex. 2.** Three measurement failures and one systematic context effect. A complete answer
distinguishes them.

| Token | What is wrong | Diagnosis | Action |
|---|---|---|---|
| **P01-003** (`keep`, IY) | F2 = **3050**, F3 = **3900**; the same speaker's other IY tokens run F2 2736/2640, F3 2982/3011. **F1 = 405 is normal.** | **Tracker skipped a formant.** What it calls F2 is really F3, and its F3 is really F4 — the tell is that everything above F1 shifted up one slot while F1 was untouched. | Re-measure with a corrected ceiling; exclude if it cannot be recovered. Log it. |
| **P02-002** (`people`, IY) | F1 = **715**; the same speaker's other IY tokens have F1 308/323. F2 = 2150 is roughly right. | **Tracker lost F1.** F1 near 700 Hz is impossible for a high front vowel (§2: high vowel ⇒ low F1). The failure is specifically at the bottom of the spectrum. | Re-measure; exclude if unrecoverable. Log it. |
| **P01-006** (`did`, IH) | duration = **0.031 s** — below the 0.05 s floor. Values (F1 545) also drift high against 494/484. | **Not enough vowel to measure.** Excluded **mechanically on duration**, regardless of whether the values look plausible. | Exclude by the stated duration criterion. No judgement call needed. |
| **P01-012 and P02-012** (`hand`, AE) | P01: F1 **700** vs 841/842, F2 **2180** vs 1939/1856. P02: F1 **570** vs 655/636, F2 **1950** vs 1668/1737. | **NOT AN ERROR.** Pre-nasal /æ/ is **raised (lower F1) and fronted (higher F2)** — and it moves the **same direction in both speakers**, which is the signature of a systematic context effect, not tracker noise. This conditioning is precisely what the lab's /æ/ indices measure. | **Keep it.** Code `following_phone = N` and let the analysis handle it. Dropping it would delete the finding. |


**Scoring note for the PI:** flagging all five as errors is the *wrong* answer, and it is the
common one. The discriminating question is not "which tokens look unusual" but "which
unusual tokens are the *instrument* failing rather than the *speaker* varying."

## Competence check *(PI-verifiable in minutes)*

- **Exercise 1 passes within tolerance** (±50 Hz F1 / ±100 Hz F2 against the RA's own
  synthesized values), and the RA can say in one sentence what a wrong ceiling did.
- **Exercise 2: the RA names the three failures and correctly declines to flag `hand`**,
  explaining that pre-nasal /æ/ raising is a property of the vowel system, not the tracker.
